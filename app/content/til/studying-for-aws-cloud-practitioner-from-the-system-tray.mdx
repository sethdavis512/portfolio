When I started studying for the AWS Certified Cloud Practitioner exam (CLF-C02), the material wasn't the problem. Finding time was. A study session meant opening a course, finding my place, and committing half an hour I rarely had.

What I did have were dozens of dead minutes a day: waiting on a build, the minute before a call, a test suite running. So I built [AWS Flashcards](https://github.com/sethdavis512/aws-flashcards), an Electron app that lives in the system tray with 130+ cards. Click the icon, flip a card or five, close it.

## Weight your cards like the exam does

CLF-C02 splits its questions across four domains, and the split isn't even:

- **Cloud Technology and Services:** 34%
- **Security and Compliance:** 30%
- **Cloud Concepts:** 24%
- **Billing, Pricing, and Support:** 12%

The deck covers all four, and a domain filter lets a short session go straight at whichever one you're weakest on. Security and Compliance plus Technology and Services are almost two-thirds of the exam, so that's where review time pays off most.

## Make it keyboard-first

If a micro-session takes more than a second to start, it doesn't happen. A click or the spacebar flips the card, keyboard shortcuts cover the rest, and each card gets marked **Know it** or **Review**. Shuffle mode keeps you from memorizing the order instead of the answers. Embla Carousel handles the swiping and transitions, so it also works fine with a trackpad.

## Progress has to survive a restart

The whole point is short, interrupted sessions, so the app has to remember where I left off. Progress lives in `localStorage`, so the cards you know and the ones that need review are still sorted the next time you open it. It's not a spaced-repetition algorithm, but sorting cards into "know it" and "review" and re-studying the review pile gets you most of the benefit.

## What I'd tell someone else studying

The tool mattered less than where it lived. Putting the cards one click away, in a place I already look a hundred times a day, turned studying from a scheduled task into something that filled gaps. If you're prepping for a certification while working full time, put your study material where your idle minutes already are.
